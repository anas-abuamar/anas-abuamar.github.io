A Local Re-Implementation of the AI Co-Scientist

2026

independent

Google published an AI co-scientist in Nature in 2026, a multi-agent system that takes a natural-language research goal and returns a tournament-ranked set of novel hypotheses. The paper ships pseudocode and per-agent prompts in its supplement. I wanted to know whether the described system actually behaves the way the paper says it does, and the only way to know that is to build it.

So this is an independent open-source re-implementation, in Python over pluggable LLM providers, following the paper’s agent roster and control flow: Generation proposes hypotheses through simulated scientific debate, Reflection reviews them for novelty and testability, Ranking runs an Elo tournament of pairwise debates, Evolution recombines and simplifies the top-ranked, Proximity embeds and clusters to drive deduplication and informative pairings, and Meta-review synthesizes the final overview. A Supervisor parses the goal into a plan and schedules the agents through a durable SQLite-backed queue with bounded concurrency.

The piece I would point to first is the benchmark log. Every cross-model bench ever run against the code is recorded with per-candidate Elo, every hypothesis produced, gold-set hits, and file pointers back to the run, generated from the bench database rather than written by hand. A multi-agent system that generates plausible text is very easy to believe and very hard to evaluate, and an honest bench trail is the only defence against grading your own homework.
