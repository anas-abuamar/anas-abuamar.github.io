Forecasting Shellfish Toxin in the Gulf of Maine

2026

Bigelow Laboratory for Ocean Sciences · ongoing

  • the current forecast, live
  • operational record and serving package (BigelowLab/pspforecast)

Paralytic shellfish toxin closes Maine’s shellfish flats. There is already a forecast that calls it, and it runs operationally: harvesters and regulators use it to decide when the flats open. I am trying to build one that is better.

The prediction problem is hard for a reason that is interesting on its own. Closures are rare. Across five seasons of the public operational record, roughly four percent of validated forecasts correspond to an actual closure. Most of the year the correct answer is “no,” which means a model can score beautifully on accuracy and be worth nothing. The operational ensemble is the line to beat, and it is a real line, not a straw man.

What I am building is a self-supervised world model for this regime: latent prediction over the driver sequences first, calibrated uncertainty second, and planning on top of that. The bet is that self-supervision earns its keep precisely where labels are scarce and the data is full of holes, which is exactly the situation here.

The part I would defend hardest is not the model, it is the evaluation. Every scientific claim in the project is backed by an automated test: leakage-safety of the temporal splits, the label-efficiency claim, calibration, and policy value are checked in CI rather than asserted in prose. If a claim breaks, the build goes red. Rare-event forecasting is unusually easy to fool yourself about, and a leakage-safe multi-seed protocol is worth as much as the architecture.

Results stay unpublished until the work is written up. The two links above are the forecast as it exists today and the public record I benchmark against, not my code.
