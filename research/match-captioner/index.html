Arabic-to-English Live Match Captioner

2026

independent

  • Try it in the browser
  • Code

Watching football in Arabic is not the same game as watching it in English. The commentary is doing something else. A good Arabic commentator will take a throw-in as an opening to tell you about the city the player is from, or a rivalry that predates everyone on the pitch, or a line of poetry that happens to fit. The match is the occasion. What you are actually being given is closer to a history lesson delivered at speed by someone who cannot stop himself.

I was watching the World Cup with friends from Japan and elsewhere, all of us in the same room watching the same match, and I realised they were watching a different one. They had the score. They did not have the digression about the player’s grandfather. The part I love most was the part that did not survive the language.

So I built a captioner. The first version captured system audio off the same laptop, fed it in ten-second chunks with four seconds of overlap to whisper-cpp for Arabic-to-English translation, and painted the result into an always-on-top window. The current one does the same thing in the browser: Whisper runs on the GPU through WebGPU, the match plays in another tab, and the captions float over it. Either way it runs entirely on the machine, no cloud API, no audio leaving the laptop. That started as a latency decision and turned out to matter more to me than the latency did.

The engineering problem is the seam. Live speech does not arrive in tidy sentences, so fixed chunks cut words in half and the translation degrades at every boundary. The overlap window exists to give the model enough context to recover across the cut, and tuning that overlap against latency is most of what makes the thing usable rather than merely working. There is a mock-audio mode that runs a recorded file through the identical path, because debugging a live pipeline on match day is not debugging.

The honest limitation is that it solves the smaller half of the problem. It translates the words. It does not carry the reference, the idiom, or the reason the commentator’s voice just went up. A literal translation of a digression about a player’s grandfather is a sentence about a grandfather. The thing worth building next is not better captions, it is annotation: surfacing what is being alluded to, not just what is being said. That is a harder problem and a more interesting one, and it is the version I actually want.
