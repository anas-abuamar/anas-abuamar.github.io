The Soil's Whisper

  1. A shared language of land
Written at UWC-USA in the autumn of 2024, after a summer on an Isleta Pueblo farm. Lightly edited for republication in 2026; the substance is unchanged.

“Rooted in Resilience, United by Soil.”

The scent of soil carries with it memories, wisdom, and the bonds that connect us to the land. That motto resonated deeply with me the summer I worked alongside the Isleta Pueblo community on a farm in New Mexico. One morning, after gathering seven pounds of beans, an older farmer beside me scooped up a handful of soil, lifted it to her face, and breathed in deeply. “I love this smell,” she said.

At that moment, I was transported back to the fields of my homeland, to my grandfather’s farm in Palestine, where his calloused hands, shaped by years of tending olive trees, taught me that soil is more than dirt: it holds our history and resilience.

In Palestine, the olive trees embody survival. They have endured harsh politics and generations of change. Working the land is a ritual that has been passed down, representing both a livelihood and a way of preserving our identity. Each harvest season, whether olive or corn, is a time to gather, reflect, and honor the land that sustains us. Through this work, I learned that to thrive, we must be deeply rooted, not just in soil but in tradition and purpose. My grandfather often said that caring for the earth is not just about what we produce; it’s about showing respect for the generations who came before us.

In New Mexico, I encountered a similar connection to land. Talking with Indigenous farmers, I heard stories of displacement, of terra nullius (land belonging to no one), and lands taken without consent. Their struggles mirrored my family’s journey in Palestine, and I felt a profound connection to this shared resilience. Just as they strive to preserve their heritage, I am driven by the responsibility to honor my roots and protect the land.

In Arabic, love is ḥubb and seed is ḥabb: the same root, ح-ب-ب, held apart by a vowel, showing how deeply love and land are intertwined, and how both need to be cared for. Through this journey, I realized that roots can extend beyond geography; they are commitments to growth and resilience that unite us across borders.

Wherever I go, I carry the strength of my heritage, grounded in a soil of connection and love that transcends place.

A shared language of land

In Palestine, harvests are acts of defiance. In New Mexico, planting is reclamation. In both, the earth speaks in the same vocabulary: patience, care, and unyielding hope.
